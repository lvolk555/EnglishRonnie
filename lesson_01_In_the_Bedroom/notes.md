## 1. Core Vocabulary

### Bed & Sleeping

- **bed** — the thing you sleep on. (床)
- **futon** — a kind of mattress that you put on the floor. English took this word from Japanese. (日式床垫)
- **mattress** — the big, soft part of the bed that you lie on. (床垫)
- **bed frame** — the hard part under the mattress that holds it up. (床架)

### Pillow

- **pillow** — a soft thing you put your head on when you sleep. (枕头)
- **pillow case** — a cover for the pillow. You can take it off and wash it. (枕套)
- **feather pillow** — a pillow filled with feathers from a duck or a goose. (羽毛枕)
- **fluffy cotton pillow** — a soft, light pillow made of cotton. (蓬松棉枕)
- **microfiber pillow** — a pillow made of very thin, fine synthetic (man-made) fibers. (超细纤维枕)

### Bedding (the cloth things on a bed)

- **sheets** — the thin cloth covers on a bed. Say the long "ee" sound: shee-ts. (床单)
- **top sheet** — the sheet that goes on top of you. (盖单)
- **fitted sheet** — the sheet on the bottom that covers the mattress. It has elastic corners so it stays on. (床垫套单/床笠)
- **blanket** — a warm cover for the bed. (毯子)
- **duvet** — a soft, thick, warm bed cover. It is a French word — say "do-vay", not "do-vet". (羽绒被)
- **comforter** — another word for a thick, warm bed cover. (厚被子)
- **quilt** — a warm bed cover made of layers of cloth, often with a pattern. (绗缝被/被子)
- **cover** — a general word for anything that keeps you warm on the bed. (被子/盖的东西)

> Note: duvet, comforter, quilt, blanket and cover are all similar — they are all things that keep you warm. The differences are small. If you want to learn them, go to a store and ask the staff to show you each one. That is also English practice!

### Furniture

- **furniture** — things in a room like beds, tables and chairs. This is an **uncountable noun** — you CANNOT say "a furniture" or "furnitures". Say "a piece of furniture". (家具)
- **night stand** / **bedside table** — a small table next to the bed. Both words mean the same thing. (床头柜)
- **dresser** — a piece of furniture with drawers, where you put clean clothes. (五斗柜/梳妆台)
- **drawers** — the sliding boxes inside a dresser. (抽屉)

### Mirror

- **mirror** — a glass thing that you look at to see yourself. (镜子)
- **reflection** — the image of yourself that you see in the mirror. (倒影/映像)

### Alarm Clock

- **alarm clock** — a clock that makes a loud noise (beep!) to wake you up in the morning. (闹钟)
- **snooze button** — the magic button on an alarm clock. It gives you 10 more minutes of sleep. (贪睡按钮/再睡一会儿按钮)

### Closet

- **closet** — a small room with a door, where you hang your clothes. (壁橱/衣柜)
- **cupboard** — another word for closet. Say "cub-berd" — the letters look different from the sound! (橱柜)
- **walk-in closet** — a very big closet that is like a whole other room. (步入式衣帽间)

## 2. Pronunciation Warnings (非常容易读错的词)

| Word | Say it like | Do NOT say |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **sheets** (床单) | shee-ts (long "ee") | shit (💩 — a very bad word!) |
| **duvet** (羽绒被) | do-vay | do-vet |
| **cupboard** (橱柜) | cub-berd | cup-board |
| **alarm clock** (闹钟) | a-larm clock (clear "l") | alarm cock (cock = penis, a bad word!) |

## 3. Grammar Notes

1. **Furniture is uncountable.** You cannot count it with numbers.
   - ❌ I have three furnitures.
   - ✅ I have three pieces of furniture.
   - ✅ The bedroom has a lot of furniture.

2. **Same meaning, different words** (同义词/近义词):
   - night stand = bedside table
   - closet = cupboard
   - duvet ≈ comforter ≈ quilt ≈ blanket ≈ cover (all keep you warm)

3. **Compound words** (合成词): English puts two words together to make a new word:
   - pillow + case = **pillow case**
   - bed + side + table = **bedside table**
   - walk + in + closet = **walk-in closet**

## 4. Useful Phrases from the Lesson

- **to go off** (for an alarm clock) — to start making noise. "My alarm clock goes off at 7:00." (闹钟响了)
- **to hit the snooze** — to press the snooze button. "I hit the snooze five times and I was late." (按贪睡按钮)
- **to fall out of bed** — to roll off the bed by accident. (从床上掉下来)
- **to roll over** — to turn your body while sleeping. (翻身)
- **to hang your clothes** — to put clothes on hangers in a closet. (挂衣服)
- **to keep you warm** — to stop you from feeling cold. (保暖)

---

## 5. Example Sentences

1. I sleep on a bed with a soft **mattress** and a **bed frame**.
2. My **pillow** is too old — I need a new **pillow case**.
3. In winter, I use a thick **duvet** (say "do-vay") to keep me warm.
4. My **alarm clock** goes off at 7:02, but I **hit the snooze** three times.
5. My clothes are in the **dresser**, and my jackets are in the **closet**.
6. I look at my **reflection** in the **mirror** every morning.
7. She is lucky — she has a **walk-in closet** in her bedroom.
8. There are many **pieces of furniture** in my bedroom: a bed, a **night stand**, and a dresser.
